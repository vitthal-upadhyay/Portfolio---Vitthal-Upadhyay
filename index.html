Instagram analytics
2 of 6

Question

What separates high-engagement Instagram posts from the rest: format, reach or posting time?

For creators and social media managers deciding what to publish.

3 of 6

Approach

Public Kaggle dataset (provenance unverified): 29,999 posts from 20 accounts. Prepared in Python on Google Colab. Engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares + saves) / impressions. A high-engagement post is one at 5.7% or above, about the top quarter. All 20 accounts were tagged as both brand and creator, so I added a reliability flag instead of trusting account type. Dashboard built in Power BI with DAX.

Limitation: Observational data of unverified origin: findings show association, not cause.
4 of 6

Finding

Post format barely matters, and reach shows almost no linear relationship with engagement rate.
4.09% / 4.08% / 4.01%
median engagement rate: image / reel / carousel
0.006
correlation between reach and engagement rate

29,999 posts from 20 accounts, Nov 2024 to Nov 2025.

5 of 6

Decision

Don't choose a format or a posting hour from averages alone. Formats were nearly identical. The best hour (2 AM, 27% high-engagement posts) beat the worst (8 AM, 23%) by 4.35 points: treat it as a lead to test, not a rule.

What I'd do differently: Verify where the data comes from before starting, and control for account, weekday and format when comparing posting hours.

Independent project / Public price data, 2000 to 2021 / about 4.7 lakh daily records, 52 symbols / Python, MySQL, Excel, Tableau

Stock market analysis
2 of 6

Question

How did Nifty 50 stocks behave through the 2020 COVID crash?

3 of 6

Approach

Python combined 52 stock files (about 4.7 lakh daily records, 2000 to 2021). MySQL calculated crash and recovery. Excel was used for further analysis. Tableau built the dashboard.

Metric definitions: Crash % compares the closing price on the first trading day of 2020 with the last trading day of March 2020. Recovery % compares the last trading day of March 2020 with the last trading day of April 2021 (April 30).
4 of 6

Finding

IndusInd Bank fell about 76% in the 2020 crash, the steepest of the stocks shown. JSW Steel recovered about 80%, the fastest.
-76%
steepest crash: IndusInd Bank (closing Jan 2020 vs March 2020)
+80%
fastest recovery: JSW Steel (March 2020 vs April 2021)
5 of 6

Decision

Evaluate market resilience through both downside exposure and recovery speed. A sharp fall and a strong rebound tell different parts of the story; neither alone establishes long-term resilience.

Limitation: Crash and recovery use fixed dates (2 Jan to 31 Mar 2020, and 31 Mar 2020 to 30 Apr 2021), not each stock's own peak and trough.
